SHARING AREAS

main problems

Every day, countless useful resources remain unused while someone else needs them. A drill may only be used a few minutes a year, a spare room can stay empty for months, and valuable skills often go unshared. Our cities waste more than materials: they also waste knowledge, time and human connections.

This system encourages everyone to buy, own and consume individually instead of cooperating. As trust between neighbours disappears, simple problems that could be solved together become expensive, isolated and inefficient.

Possible Solutions

The sharing economy unlocks unused resources. By creating spaces where people exchange goods, services and knowledge, a city can reduce waste while strengthening its community.

Existing initiatives already prove that this works:

  • Time banks: one hour of tutoring exchanged for one hour of gardening.
  • Repair workshops: learn to fix objects instead of replacing them.
  • Community gardens: cultivate and share food together.
  • Libraries of Things: borrow tools instead of buying them.
  • Crowdfunding: collectively finance projects that benefit everyone.
Sharing spaces make everyday life cheaper, more sustainable and more social by replacing individual ownership with cooperation whenever possible.
